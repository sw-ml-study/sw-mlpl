This is the 1:1 companion to the playground's *MLX tic-tac-toe fine-tune*
demo. We fine-tune a tiny board-state policy on the Apple GPU and then
*demonstrate* the result the honest way: by playing 100 games against a
random opponent and counting wins/ties/losses, *before* and *after*. No
"trust me, the loss dropped" -- you watch the piles of losses turn into
piles of wins. The blocks share one ~:session ttt~, so state threads
through exactly as if typed into the REPL.

* Step 1 -- the dataset: every position, labelled with its best move

~ttt_boards()~ enumerates every reachable non-terminal position (deduped,
from the side-to-move's perspective) and one-hot-encodes each of the nine
cells as ~[empty, mine, theirs]~ -- 27 features. ~ttt_moves()~ gives the
matching label: the optimal move from a minimax solver. Together they are
the complete supervised policy.

X = ttt_boards()
Y = ttt_moves()
