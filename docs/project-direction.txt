Revised understanding of sw-MLPL
The earlier roadmap understated the project’s current maturity.

The most important corrections are:

Engram is no longer merely planned. E1 primitives, E2 DSL/training support, and E3 Tiny-LM integration are complete. The implementation includes deterministic n-gram addressing, trainable memory, tape differentiation, in-chain insertion after attention, frozen-base training, collision statistics, gate statistics, and end-to-end demos.

MLX is not just an external service or CPU-backed experiment anymore. Metal has been enabled, a backend-neutral TensorHandle abstraction exists, lazy MLX tensors remain resident through the forward tape, and gradients now remain resident during backward for the principal operation set.

The active AgentRail saga is E4, currently at step 6, not an early MLX implementation milestone. The next work is optimizer residency, parity, benchmarking, and cleanup—not creating an MLX backend from scratch.

sw-MLPL already has substantial notebook/research infrastructure: datasets, tokenization, experiment tracking, autograd, optimizers, training blocks, model DSL, compilation to Rust, visualization, server sessions, SSE training telemetry, cancellation, remote MLX execution, LoRA, feasibility estimation, and LLM calls. The older docs/status.md captures many of these completed sagas, although its “next saga” section has clearly fallen behind the active E-series work.

CUDA is also not completely greenfield. CUDA/Candle crates exist and are target-gated; the outstanding work is Linux validation, resident tensor integration, backend completeness, and performance—not simply introducing a CUDA feature flag.

The current E4 plan confirms that the key architectural change is a device-aware autograd tape and resident optimizer state, with MLX performance—not mere functional parity—as the acceptance criterion.

The strongest missing areas are not additional model architectures. They are the techniques that determine whether small models actually become useful: data generation, curriculum, distillation, evaluation, quantization, and causal inspection.

Highest-priority additions
1. Data-centric training and synthetic curriculum
This is the largest gap in the current roadmap.

The small-model material repeatedly points toward the conclusion that model size alone is a poor predictor of capability. TinyLlama-style overtraining and Phi-style synthetic textbook data show that data quantity, quality, structure, and curriculum can compensate substantially for fewer parameters. 

The multi-hop reasoning work demonstrates an even more actionable pattern:

Generate structured reasoning problems.

Use a knowledge graph or other oracle to validate solutions.

Reject incorrect or poorly formed examples.

Train the small model with scaffolding.

Remove the scaffold at inference.

Test against the actual target distribution.

The reported demonstration takes a 135M model from no useful multi-hop performance to much stronger task accuracy through knowledge-graph-guided training and rejection sampling. The broader lesson is that training-data construction is itself an ML algorithm.

2. Distillation as a first-class research facility
Distillation appears indirectly in the small-model material, particularly in discussion of models such as Gemma, but it has not yet been decomposed into an executable sw-MLPL plan. 

The old Saga 18 mentioned distillation, but bundled it with ICL, ICRL, Engram, and orchestration. That bundle should remain split.

Distillation is particularly aligned with your objectives because it lets:

a slow, capable model create training targets

a small model learn the behavior

CPU or older-GPU systems run the resulting student

agent trajectories become reusable training material

MTP heads learn from a stronger teacher

tool use transfer from large models to compact local models

Distillation variants worth supporting
Logit distillation
student loss =
    α * hard_target_loss
  + β * KL(student_logits, teacher_logits)
Sequence distillation
The teacher generates a cleaned or simplified target sequence, and the student learns that sequence.

Feature distillation
Intermediate student states are encouraged to approximate teacher representations.

Policy or trajectory distillation
The student learns:

tool choices

action sequences

reflections

corrections

successful agent trajectories

Self-distillation
A model teaches:

a smaller copy

an earlier checkpoint

its own MTP heads

a draft model

Distillation with rejection sampling
Generate many teacher solutions, validate them objectively, and train only on accepted solutions.

This is especially attractive for Rust tasks because compilation and tests provide cheap, deterministic verification.

3. Rejection sampling and verifier-guided learning
Rejection sampling deserves its own capability rather than being hidden inside the data pipeline.

The multi-hop series uses structured verification to select good reasoning examples, and the broader agent roadmap needs exactly the same pattern. 

General form:

sample N candidates
        ↓
evaluate each candidate
        ↓
accept / rank / weight
        ↓
use for inference, ICL, or training
This supports three modes.

Inference-time best-of-N
Generate several answers and choose one.

Dataset generation
Generate candidate examples, retain valid ones, and train on them.

Agent self-improvement
Store high-reward trajectories and reject misleading or reward-hacked ones.

sw-MLPL primitives
samples = sample_many(model, prompt, n=16)

scored = evaluate_many(samples, verifier)

best = select_best(scored)
accepted = filter_reward(scored, min_reward=0.8)
Needed features:

parallel candidate generation

deterministic evaluators

multiple reward components

ranking

diversity penalties

acceptance thresholds

weighted examples

provenance from candidate to checkpoint

compute-budget accounting

Where it belongs
Implement the generic rejection/ranking substrate with data-forge-1, then reuse it in:

MTP proposal analysis

multi-hop training

ICRL

trajectory tuning

distillation

recursive-model voting

This is a cross-cutting primitive, not a single demo.

8. Energy-based models and associative memory
The general ML playlist’s energy-based lineage—from Hopfield networks through Boltzmann machines to JEPA—introduces an important conceptual family that is currently absent from the roadmap. The key shared idea is learning a scalar compatibility or energy function, then finding states or representations with low energy. 

This is relevant to sw-MLPL in three ways.

Associative memory
Hopfield-style memory complements Engram:

Engram	Hopfield-style memory
Deterministic hash address	Content-addressed retrieval
Exact slot lookup	Attractor convergence
Sparse row access	Iterative state refinement
Fast known-pattern retrieval	Noise-tolerant recall
A compact demo could compare:

hash-based Engram retrieval

nearest-neighbor retrieval

classic Hopfield recall

modern continuous Hopfield retrieval

Energy-based evaluation
Agent outputs or candidate plans could be ranked by a learned compatibility score rather than autoregressive likelihood alone.

Recursive inference
Energy minimization is another form of test-time computation and connects naturally to TRM/HRM-style iterative refinement.

Where it belongs
After the generic recursive runtime, not before MTP or ICRL:

recursive-runtime
    ├── TRM
    ├── HRM
    └── energy-minimization demo
Priority
Medium-low, but educationally valuable.

This does not directly serve near-term language-model acceleration, but it would broaden sw-MLPL beyond supervised classifiers and autoregressive language models.

Main conclusion
The playlists suggest that the current roadmap is still too model-feature oriented.

The most consequential missing layer is:

How do we create, validate, select, sequence,
evaluate, and reuse learning experiences?
That layer connects all four of your priorities:

Speed: MTP and draft models need properly constructed training data and meaningful Pareto evaluation.

Agent quality: ICL and ICRL need validated trajectories, selection policies, curriculum, and rejection sampling.

Colab replacement: serious ML experimentation requires dataset generation, sweeps, robustness testing, and reproducible reports.

Small/old hardware: distillation, quantization, data quality, and architecture efficiency are what make small models practically capable.

I would therefore elevate data-forge + rejection sampling + curriculum + distillation to the same strategic level as MTP and ICRL. Those are the clearest important concepts represented in the playlists that were not yet adequately covered in the sw-MLPL plan.
