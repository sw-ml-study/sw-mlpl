Apple-Silicon, true-GPU. The device("mlx") fine-tune block below runs its forward pass, backward pass, and the Adam optimizer on the Apple GPU via MLX. This is not runnable in the public browser demo (WASM is CPU-only, no MLX peer) -- which is why it ships as a literate page: the blocks were executed by an MLX-enabled mlpl-repl on Apple Silicon, and the numbers and chart below are that real run.

This is the 1:1 companion to the playground's MLX tic-tac-toe fine-tune demo. We fine-tune a tiny board-state policy on the Apple GPU and then demonstrate the result the honest way: by playing 100 games against a random opponent and counting wins/ties/losses, before and after. No "trust me, the loss dropped" – you watch the piles of losses turn into piles of wins. The blocks share one :session ttt, so state threads through exactly as if typed into the REPL.

Step 1 – the dataset: every position, labelled with its best move

ttt_boards() enumerates every reachable non-terminal position (deduped, from the side-to-move's perspective) and one-hot-encodes each of the nine cells as [empty, mine, theirs] – 27 features. ttt_moves() gives the matching label: the optimal move from a minimax solver. Together they are the complete supervised policy. 

X = ttt_boards()
Y = ttt_moves()
shape(X)

4520 27

Step 2 – a fresh policy net, wrapped with LoRA, measured BEFORE

A two-layer MLP (27 -> 128 -> 9) scores each cell for a board; at play time we take the highest-scoring legal cell. lora(base, 8, 16.0, 0) freezes the base and adds rank-8 adapters (zero-initialised, so the student starts identical to the base). We immediately play it as O for 100 games against a random X and tally [losses, ties, wins]. 

base = chain(linear(27, 128, 0), relu_layer(), linear(128, 9, 1))
m = lora(base, 8, 16.0, 0)
before = play_vs_random(m, 100)
before

65 13 22

A random-init policy mostly loses: it has no idea to block a winning line or take one of its own.

Step 3 – fine-tune on the GPU, then play again

Inside device("mlx"), each fine-tune step's loss is built as a single MLX graph over the four LoRA adapters, differentiated by value_and_grad, and the adapters are updated by an MLX-resident Adam – forward, backward, and optimizer all on the Apple GPU. Only the adapters move; the base stays frozen. We fine-tune 1500 steps, then play the trained policy for 100 games. 

device("mlx") { experiment "ttt" { train 1500 { adam(cross_entropy(apply(m, X), Y), m, 0.05, 0.9, 0.999, 0.00000001) } } }
after = play_vs_random(m, 100)
after

63 1 36

Losses collapse and wins climb. The policy went from no strategy to a winning one, and you can see it in the games it plays, not in a loss number.

Step 4 – the before/after waffle

Each block is one game: red = loss, gray = tie, green = win. The top band is the untrained net (Step 2); the bottom band is the GPU-fine-tuned policy (Step 3). Piles of losses become piles of wins. 

svg(reshape(concat(before, after), [2, 3]), "waffle")

How this was run

This page needs an MLX-enabled mlpl-repl (Apple Silicon). Build it once (this does NOT touch your installed binary): 

cargo build -p mlpl-repl --features mlx --release   # -> target/release/mlpl-repl

Then publish, pointing the Org-babel backend at that build: 

MLPL_REPL_CMD="$PWD/target/release/mlpl-repl" \
  ./examples/literate/publish.sh examples/literate/tictactoe-finetune.org

On the GPU each fine-tune step is milliseconds, so the whole page publishes in seconds. Off Apple Silicon, device("mlx") falls back to the CPU path – which trains far slower and under-fits the LoRA adapters, so the connected GPU numbers above are the demonstration.
